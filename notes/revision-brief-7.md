## 2. The substitute king — «الملك البديل» (author's idea; the novel's main PLOT TWIST)
History (footnote it once, in chunk A, key `a7_shar_puhi`): in Mesopotamian astrology, when an eclipse (above all a lunar eclipse) portended the king's death, a **substitute king (Akkadian šar pūḫi)** was enthroned for the danger period (up to ~100 days), given the regalia and a queen, while the real king hid as "the farmer"; when the period ended the substitute was put to death so the omen was fulfilled on him. Assyrian letters to Esarhaddon (7th c. BC) document it. The idea passed through Harran's star-worshipping scholars into the Arabic astrological literature (the novel may claim al-Hakim read it in a Harranian book in دار الحكمة).
The twist: **al-Hakim had been preparing Saif — the man born the same night, with the same name and the same number 386 — as his substitute king.** The census of every «منصور» born in 375 was a search for substitutes; protecting Saif, letting him flee south, knowing where he was every month, the prophecy «فإن كانا اثنين، لم يبقَ إلا واحد» — all of it was to keep the perfect substitute alive until the shadow came. Ibn Yunus understood it early, and that is why he RENAMED Mansur «سيف»: to take him out of the rite, which needs the same name (but the number remained…).
